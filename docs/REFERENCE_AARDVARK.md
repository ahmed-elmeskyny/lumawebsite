# Aardvark Reference Analysis for Luma

This document defines how the Luma website may use Aardvark Book Club as a
strategic reference without copying it.

Use it together with:

- `CLAUDE.md`
- `BRAND.md`
- `PRODUCTS.md`
- `CONTENT.md`
- `SITE_BRIEF.md`

This is a design-reference document, not permission to reproduce another
website. When this document and another approved source-of-truth file overlap,
the more specific Luma rule takes priority.

## Status

This document is a review draft until the user approves it.

After approval:

- Treat the adaptation and originality rules as binding.
- Use Aardvark only to understand high-level design principles.
- Translate every useful principle through Luma's own products, packaging,
  mascot, colors, fonts, patterns, copy, and commerce model.
- Do not use Aardvark as a layout template.
- Do not start implementation from screenshots of the reference.

## Executive Direction

The correct relationship between the two brands is:

> Aardvark demonstrates how a small catalogue can feel like a rich,
> collectible world. Luma should apply that confidence to socks while making
> product choice and purchase faster, clearer, and more mobile-friendly.

Luma should adapt:

- Expressive editorial hierarchy
- Strong product storytelling
- A clear world around a compact catalogue
- Bold but controlled section-to-section color changes
- Product imagery as a central visual element
- Short, characterful labels and annotations
- A memorable packaging reveal
- A simple explanation of how the offer works
- Mood-led discovery

Luma must improve:

- Speed from landing to a product
- Visibility of price and product type
- Visibility of size selection and availability
- Visibility of cash-on-delivery reassurance
- Reachability of the primary cart action
- Mobile simplicity
- Motion fallbacks
- Page length and content discipline

Luma must never copy:

- Source code
- Copywriting
- Illustrations
- Icons
- Fonts
- Logos
- Photography
- Product imagery
- Exact colors
- Exact layouts
- Distinctive shapes
- Section compositions
- Transition drawings
- Animation choreography
- Membership language
- Book-club mechanics

## What the Reference Does Well

### 1. It leads with a distinct point of view

The homepage does not look like a generic commerce template. A large display
headline, confident color field, compact supporting statement, and product
visual establish the brand immediately.

**Principle for Luma:** make the first viewport recognizably Luma before the
visitor reads every word.

**Luma translation:**

- Lead with **“Wear who you are.”**
- Use the approved combined-editions render or another approved product asset.
- Use one controlled Luma color field or neutral field.
- Keep the support line short.
- Show `Shop Luma` as the primary action and `Explore the editions` as the
  secondary path.

Do not imitate the reference headline arrangement, typeface, yellow field,
box placement, or entrance animation.

### 2. It gives a small catalogue editorial weight

Aardvark presents a limited monthly selection as something curated and
collectible. Large imagery, descriptive tags, short summaries, and generous
space help each item feel important.

**Principle for Luma:** six sellable launch products are enough when each one
has a clear identity.

**Luma translation:**

- Give each edition and standalone design a distinct visual field.
- Use approved mood tags from `CONTENT.md`.
- Let sock artwork determine a card's color direction.
- Show enough of the real product to identify the design.
- Keep product name, product type, price, and availability understandable.

Do not hide commerce facts behind hover, animation, or a `Read more` interaction.

### 3. It uses color to create chapters

The homepage alternates strong color fields and quieter sections. This gives a
long page rhythm and makes each major idea feel like a new chapter.

**Principle for Luma:** color should organize the experience, not decorate it
randomly.

**Luma translation:**

- Use a controlled subset of the palette within each section.
- Alternate expressive product-led sections with calm Eggshell, Luma White, or
  Crystal breathing spaces.
- Let Color Your Steps and Healthy Shifts use different edition-led accents.
- Use Onyx for primary text on suitable light fields.
- Test every text/background combination for contrast.

Do not use every Luma color in one viewport or recreate Aardvark's exact color
sequence.

### 4. It explains the offer visually

The reference uses a numbered how-it-works sequence with custom illustrations
and concise supporting text.

**Principle for Luma:** explain unfamiliar product and purchase rules before
they cause uncertainty.

**Luma translation:**

Use the approved three-step message:

1. Pick your mood.
2. Choose your size.
3. Pay when it arrives.

The sequence must make clear that:

- Standalone products contain one pair.
- Editions contain three predetermined pairs.
- A single size applies to all three pairs in an edition.
- Payment is cash on delivery.

Use approved mascot poses, sock-derived graphics, or packaging assets. Do not
copy the reference's illustrations, step layout, numbering style, or wording.

### 5. It turns packaging into part of the experience

The Aardvark box is not treated as shipping waste. It reinforces the identity
and becomes part of the story.

**Principle for Luma:** the rigid edition box is a product feature and a
collectible brand surface.

**Luma translation:**

- Use the approved open, closed, top-down, and filled-box renders.
- Show the correct artwork and three designs for each edition.
- Allow one short box-opening or lid-reveal moment.
- Explain that editions arrive in a rigid magnetic box.
- Keep standalone zipper-bag packaging visually distinct.

Do not imply that a rigid box is included with standalone socks.

### 6. It uses tags as fast emotional summaries

The reference product page uses short tags and an “at a glance” treatment to
communicate tone before the long description.

**Principle for Luma:** mood can be understood faster than a long product
paragraph.

**Luma translation:**

- Use only approved mood tags.
- Keep tags short and readable.
- Treat tags as editorial metadata unless real Shopify tags or metafields
  support filtering.
- Pair mood language with literal product facts.

Do not copy the reference's emoji system, tag shapes, wording, or order.

### 7. It combines browsing with storytelling

The reference product page moves from product identification to an emotional
summary, longer narrative, social proof, sample content, notes, and related
products.

**Principle for Luma:** a product page can sell the design's personality as
well as the physical item.

**Luma translation:**

- Put the purchase decision first.
- Use the approved product story after the essential buying controls.
- For editions, explain the three included designs and box.
- Use approved related products only after the main product is understood.
- Keep unverified materials, construction, care, or quality claims unpublished.

Luma must not use invented reviews, press quotes, ratings, testimonials, or
social proof.

## Reference Homepage Analysis

### Observed structure

The current reference homepage contains:

1. Fixed navigation
2. Large branded hero
3. Current product selection
4. How-it-works explanation
5. Packaging and membership story
6. Category or genre discovery
7. Benefits
8. FAQ
9. Gifting
10. Press and member-recognition content
11. Exclusive product story
12. Newsletter and footer

Its strength is variety and rhythm. Its risk for Luma is length. Luma has six
sellable launch products, no membership, no verified press, no approved
customer reviews, and no live gifting program.

### Luma homepage implication

Use the approved order from `SITE_BRIEF.md`:

1. Announcement bar
2. Navigation
3. “Wear who you are.” hero
4. Featured editions
5. Packaging reveal
6. Three-step explanation
7. Mood discovery
8. Featured standalone pairs
9. Product-expression section
10. Mascot-led brand story
11. Verified customer-content invitation, if ready
12. Approved FAQ preview
13. Newsletter, only if operationally ready
14. Footer

Adjacent sections may be combined when it improves pace. Do not add content
only to reproduce the reference's length.

## Reference Product-Page Analysis

### What is useful

The current product reference uses:

- A very large product name
- Category or mood labels
- One dominant product image
- A quick emotional summary
- Longer narrative content
- Supporting notes
- Related products

This creates a strong editorial identity around one product.

### What Luma must change

The reference is not a standard direct-purchase product page. Its main action
can appear below the initial desktop viewport and it hands off to a membership
experience.

Luma must not reproduce that hierarchy.

On mobile, the Luma purchase area must prioritize:

1. Main product image
2. Product name and product type
3. Live Shopify price
4. Required size selection
5. Quantity
6. Cash-on-delivery and 35 MAD delivery message
7. `Add to cart`

Do not preselect a size. Do not make essential purchase controls depend on a
sticky bar, animation, horizontal carousel, accordion, or hover.

After the purchase area:

- Show approved mood tags and product story.
- For an edition, show the exact three designs, fixed-content rule,
  one-size-for-all rule, and box presentation.
- For a standalone pair, show the zipper bag and clearly state that the rigid
  box is not included.
- Show size-guide access near the selector.
- Show materials, care, delivery time, and policy details only after approval.

## Navigation Direction

### Principle to adapt

The reference keeps its primary navigation compact and gives the brand mark a
clear role.

### Luma implementation direction

Desktop navigation:

- Logo linked to the homepage
- Shop
- Editions
- Our story
- Size guide
- FAQ
- Search only if implemented
- Cart with an accurate item count

Mobile navigation:

- Visible logo
- Clear menu control
- Visible cart
- No essential destination hidden behind animated labels
- No social icons competing with shopping actions

The announcement bar should state only approved delivery and COD information.
Do not add a countdown, promotion, or free-shipping claim.

## Product-Card Direction

Each Luma product card should communicate without interaction:

- Product image
- Product name
- `Edition · 3 pairs` or `Standalone · 1 pair`
- Live Shopify price
- Availability state when relevant

Optional editorial elements:

- One or two approved mood tags
- A short annotation
- A collection-led color field
- A subtle approved mascot detail

Interaction may add:

- Small image shift
- Controlled tilt
- Color-field change
- Secondary image reveal

The card must remain understandable when:

- Hover is unavailable
- Motion is disabled
- The secondary image has not loaded
- A product is sold out

Do not copy Aardvark's card proportions, typography, hover choreography,
tag shapes, slider controls, or image treatment.

## Original Luma Visual Translation

### Typography

Use:

- BaileywickJF Gothic for short, high-impact display moments
- Adelphi PE Variable for interface, body, prices, forms, and product facts

Adapt the principle of dramatic scale contrast, but create an original type
system. Do not recreate Aardvark's line breaks, compressed headline
proportions, font sizing ratios, or letter-by-letter effects.

### Color

Use only the approved Luma palette in `BRAND.md`.

Recommended rhythm:

- One confident hero field
- Calm neutral area for product comparison
- Edition-led color chapters
- Quiet product-information area
- One expressive mascot or story section
- Calm footer and operational content

This is a rhythm recommendation, not a fixed color assignment. Final
combinations require contrast checks with the real assets and copy.

### Shapes and graphic language

Create Luma-specific shapes from:

- Sock cuffs
- Ribbing
- Stitch paths
- Heel and toe curves
- Folded sock silhouettes
- Packaging panels
- Tape, labels, and approved stickers
- Existing product patterns
- Approved mascot gestures

Do not use Aardvark's distinctive curves, blobs, logo paths, book shapes,
frames, or transition strokes.

### Handwritten annotations

Handwritten or hand-drawn annotations may be used sparingly for:

- Mood labels
- Packaging callouts
- Mascot reactions
- Small directional cues

They must not replace:

- Prices
- Sizes
- Product names
- Form labels
- Error messages
- Delivery or payment information

Use an approved asset or an approved type treatment. Do not invent an
additional font.

### Mascot

The Luma mascot can:

- Point toward an edition
- React to a box opening
- Introduce the three-step explanation
- Support an empty-cart or brand-story moment
- Add one small detail to a section transition

The mascot must not:

- Obscure product artwork
- Pull attention from `Add to cart`
- Move continuously on mobile
- Appear in every section
- Be redrawn to imitate the reference's character
- Default to medical styling outside Healthy Shifts

## Motion Direction

### Reference lesson

The reference uses a full-screen branded transition and characterful movement
to make navigation memorable.

### Luma adaptation

Luma may use one short first-visit box-opening reveal:

- Maximum duration: 1.2 seconds
- Static equivalent available immediately
- Skipped for repeat visitors
- Skipped or simplified for `prefers-reduced-motion`
- Must fail open if animation code or an asset does not load
- Must not delay navigation or product interaction

Approved motion purposes:

- Reveal a box lid
- Clarify that a card is interactive
- Confirm a size or cart action
- Transition a mascot into a story moment
- Add gentle depth to product imagery

Avoid:

- Scroll-jacking
- Long transition covers
- Letter-by-letter animation on essential copy
- Motion required to reveal price or controls
- Continuous marquee movement on mobile
- Multiple simultaneous parallax layers
- Autoplay movement that cannot settle
- Heavy animation on every section

GSAP is allowed only if the approved box-opening sequence cannot be implemented
reliably with Motion for React or CSS.

## Mobile-First Corrections

Aardvark's expressive desktop scale is a useful confidence reference, but Luma
must be designed from the 390 px mobile condition first.

At 390×844:

- Keep the hero message and primary action within the first useful viewport.
- Ensure the combined-editions image remains readable at a small size.
- Avoid headline line breaks that create isolated one-word rows.
- Keep product type and price visible on cards.
- Use swipeable content only when controls and progress are understandable.
- Do not require horizontal dragging to discover all essential products.
- Keep touch targets appropriately sized.
- Keep cart access visible.
- Prevent decorative layers from creating horizontal overflow.
- Avoid continuous product or mascot animation.
- Preserve a logical heading and reading order.

At tablet and desktop:

- Increase editorial scale without hiding commerce facts.
- Use whitespace to separate chapters.
- Keep maximum text widths readable.
- Do not stretch mobile cards into empty oversized panels.
- Let approved transparent product imagery create depth.

## Morocco and COD Corrections

The reference sells a subscription-like membership in the USA and Canada.
Luma sells physical products in Morocco through Shopify with cash on delivery.

Therefore, Luma must not adapt:

- Membership language
- Monthly-drop mechanics
- `Join the club` calls to action
- `Build your box` language
- Subscription pricing
- App-download prompts
- Shipping claims
- Gifting mechanics not actually offered

Luma must communicate:

- Price in MAD
- 80 MAD for one standalone pair
- 235 MAD for a fixed three-pair edition
- 35 MAD delivery
- EU 36–40 and EU 41–46
- One size for every pair inside an edition
- Cash on delivery
- Shopify-hosted checkout

Operational facts must be direct and unambiguous even when the surrounding
design is playful.

## Accessibility Direction

Useful reference energy must not weaken usability.

The Luma design must:

- Use semantic page structure.
- Preserve visible keyboard focus.
- Use text labels for essential controls.
- Provide descriptive alt text for meaningful product imagery.
- Use empty alt text for purely decorative art.
- Avoid conveying state through color alone.
- Meet appropriate contrast requirements.
- Keep interactive targets appropriately sized.
- Announce cart and validation changes accessibly.
- Preserve logical focus when menus and cart panels open or close.
- Keep the storefront usable when animation is disabled.

Decorative handwritten text embedded in imagery must not be the only source of
essential information.

## Performance Direction

The reference demonstrates the appeal of rich visual storytelling, but it also
shows the risk of a long, media-heavy experience.

For Luma:

- Prioritize the product-discovery and purchase path.
- Use responsive WebP or AVIF delivery copies.
- Preserve transparent PNG masters outside runtime delivery.
- Define image dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- Preload only the critical hero asset when justified.
- Lazy-load below-fold box, mascot, and product media.
- Load only required font files and weights.
- Avoid duplicate desktop and mobile media downloads.
- Avoid a large animation dependency for simple movement.
- Do not block the page behind an intro.
- Test on a realistically constrained mobile connection.

Performance budgets and exact pass criteria belong in
`ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md`.

## Direction Unsuitable for Launch

The following ideas may be visually attractive but are unsuitable for the
current Luma launch:

- A long cinematic intro
- An extremely long homepage built to imitate the reference's section count
- A product page that delays price, size, COD, or `Add to cart`
- A carousel as the only way to access products
- Mood filters without approved Shopify data
- A build-your-own-box interaction
- A membership or subscription model
- Fake customer or press content
- Newsletter capture before consent and privacy requirements are ready
- Desktop-first typography that breaks mobile reading
- Continuous mascot or decorative motion
- Heavy animation that competes with Shopify data loading

## Review Checklist for Claude

Before presenting the Luma visual direction, Claude must answer:

1. Does this look recognizably Luma without naming the reference?
2. Are products, product type, price, and purchase paths clearer than on the
   reference?
3. Does every adapted principle use approved Luma assets and rules?
4. Is any composition, shape, font treatment, or animation too close to the
   reference?
5. Does the 390×844 design work without hover or motion?
6. Are COD, delivery charge, size, and fixed-edition rules easy to find?
7. Are all product images accurate?
8. Is the mascot supporting the task rather than competing with it?
9. Can the page work if the intro, motion library, or secondary media fails?
10. Has any unapproved claim, review, filter, policy, or feature been added?

If any answer is uncertain, revise the proposal or report the issue before
implementation.
